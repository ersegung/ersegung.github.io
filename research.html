Research

I study the coupled dynamics of fluids and structures, with a particular interest in flexible bodies, nonlinear response, and the physics that govern loading and vibration.

Flow-induced vibrations

Flexible offshore structures can vibrate as vortices form and shed in the surrounding flow. My research examines how structural modes, boundary conditions, and fluid forcing combine to produce these motions. I am particularly interested in modal interactions, hysteresis, and the transfer of energy between a structure and its wake.

Marine renewable energy

My work on wave energy connects device hydrodynamics with structural response and energy capture. Research topics include oscillating water columns, oscillating wave surge converters, and flexible structures that modify wave loading. I collaborate with the Hawaiʻi Marine Energy Center on experimental and numerical studies of marine energy systems.

Wave–ice–structure interactions

Waves, floating ice, and offshore structures interact through bending, collisions, and changing contact conditions. I investigate these processes using laboratory measurements, hydroelastic models, and multivariate analysis. Related work considers ship activity and sea-ice conditions in Alaskan waters.

Data-driven dynamics and monitoring

I use modal decomposition, system identification, and reduced-order models to connect complex measurements with physical behavior. Applications include identifying structural and wake modes, reconstructing environmental loads, and tracking changes in mechanical systems.

Experiments and computation

Our work combines water-channel and wave-flume experiments with numerical fluid–structure interaction models. High-speed imaging, motion tracking, and force measurements allow us to study the response of structures and test the assumptions behind our models.
